# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

native

Desktop app for macOS, Windows, and Linux. The window follows the system light and dark theme.

## Users

People who need to inspect a website with a desktop SEO spider. They are at a desk with one site to check. The job is the crawl and the issues, not a walkthrough.

## Product Purpose

The app crawls one host and shows the checks that crawl first: internal URLs, page titles, meta descriptions, H1s, response codes (broken links and redirects), canonicals, image alt text, internal links, and the URLs a site's sitemaps list. It can read the server's HTML alone or render each page first in a locally installed browser, and it keeps the two captures distinct. Success is seeing those facts for the pages the crawl actually reached and a truthful account of every sitemap URL it did not check.

## Positioning

The app serves people who need a desktop crawl followed by on-page, response, and sitemap checks. Its shipped features cover on-page, response, canonical, image, internal-link, and sitemap discovery and auditing; it is not a full crawler.

## Operating Context

A native window. Paste a URL, leave **Use sitemap URLs** on to include URLs listed in sitemap files, choose **HTML** or **Browser** for page rendering, press Crawl, then start on **Overview** and switch to a table when you want the rows. Turning the sitemap option off still follows page links and respects robots.txt rules. The results toolbar searches and filters to All, Findings or Incomplete rows for the current view. Select a result to open its contextual inspector; copy a full value, inspect a related in-dataset URL, move to the previous or next visible result, or open the page in a browser. The Sitemaps tab has URLs and Files views over the same crawl. The footer states the crawl result, including when the page cap stopped it. `cargo run` opens the window. On launch and every 24 hours the app checks a signed release manifest; an available update is offered in a strip and installed only when the person asks to restart.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed in the current app:

- Same host only. Other hosts are not followed.
- HTML is the default capture and reads only the response the server sends. Browser is an optional capture that renders each page first in a locally installed Chrome or Chromium, executing its scripts before the content audits run. Browser mode never downloads a browser, attaches to the user's everyday profile, or disables the browser sandbox or TLS verification. If no browser is found or its controlled launch fails, the app says so in a short line, offers **Retry**, **Choose browser…**, and **Use HTML**, and keeps Crawl disabled until one can launch. Raw launch diagnostics stay behind **Details** with copy access. An explicit choice is kept for recovery even when it goes stale.
- Browser setup speaks in applications: on macOS **Choose browser…** opens a normal Open panel in `/Applications` where `Google Chrome.app` is selectable directly, and the explicit choice is remembered between launches. Browser mode runs one managed browser process per crawl with a fresh isolated profile and context per page, at most two pages at once, and a 15-second deadline per page covering navigation, a one-second post-load settle, and extraction. Resource work is bounded (300 requests and 50 MiB per page); reaching a cap is stated, not hidden. HTML mode keeps its existing parallel request pool and does not start a browser.
- Defaults are 40 pages and 3 levels deep (the seed is level 0). The page limit has no fixed maximum; crawl size depends on the limit you enter and the computer's available resources. Depth can be set up to 10.
- Does not implement login flows, form submission, clicking, consent handling, scrolling or infinite-scroll crawling, mobile emulation, custom user JavaScript, or screenshot capture. Browser resources may load assets and APIs from other hosts while rendering, but only in-scope document navigations become crawl pages.
- Reads robots.txt (Allow, Disallow, Crawl-delay) and applies the same scope, robots, redirect, page-limit, and sitemap rules to Browser pages before a document navigation is allowed. A refused in-scope redirect or a later navigation hands its destination to the crawl queue instead of hiding the hop; an out-of-scope navigation is blocked and recorded. Indexability reads every generic and Googlebot robots meta declaration and every `X-Robots-Tag` header line, keeping their raw text, multiplicity, and agent scope. Other agent rules are shown as scoped evidence and are not applied as generic or Google Web Search rules. Raw evidence is bounded per page; when the cap is reached, an applicable noindex or nofollow in the omitted tail is still retained as a flag and the assessment states that evidence was truncated.
- Browser capture provenance is kept separate from the audited document: source-HTML declarations, rendered declarations, and their capture stage are labeled in the drawer, and a script-removed source noindex or a script-changed canonical stays visible as a change across captures. A rendered canonical that differs from the source is called out rather than treated as a duplicate declaration. A timed-out, blocked, cancelled, failed, or truncated render is an incomplete capture and never becomes “No indexing block detected”; the same applies when a script required by the page fails to load, because the rendered page may be missing content that script would have added. Its row, drawer, sitemap entry, and CSV say what is missing.
- Fetches run in parallel and stream into the table as they land; Stop cancels the crawl and the action reads Stopping until the worker finishes. Transient 429/5xx/transport failures retry with backoff, and a 429/503 pauses the pool.
- Starting a crawl validates the URL, page budget and depth together, whether started by the button or a keyboard submit. Invalid input shows a concise field error and keeps the previous results and their run context intact; Browser mode also refuses to start without a usable browser. Each run keeps its own seed, host, limits, capture mode and sitemap setting, available from the results toolbar's **Run** details.
- Results support a case-insensitive literal search over URL, title, meta description, H1, canonical, content type and displayed finding text, plus a three-state filter: All, Findings and Incomplete. Incompleteness and a confirmed observation can coexist: a retained noindex, observed HTTP error or broken link remains a finding when the capture is incomplete, while completed title/meta/H1/image claims stay withheld. An incomplete check never becomes a clean row. Search, filter, sort and selection are remembered per tab and per Sitemaps URLs/Files view, and a new crawl clears transient searches without changing the next-run options.
- Overview is the default entry point. It shows the saved run's host, captured URL count, actual capture mode and terminal state; nonzero finding groups for responses, indexing/canonical review, titles, descriptions, headings, image alt text and broken links; and separate coverage for incomplete captures, incomplete sitemap URL checks, and sitemap file findings. Group counts are per check and may overlap, so they are never summed into a unique-page total. Overview has no URL search, sort or copy controls; **Open URL table** and **Export CSV** keep the table one click away, and Cmd/Ctrl+F moves to Internal search. A stopped, limited or incomplete run is not presented as an all-clear.
- The results toolbar shows visible versus total URLs for the active view, known findings and incomplete checks. Zero visible rows distinguish no crawl, preparing, a failed crawl, no sitemap results, no findings in this view and no search matches; filtered-empty states offer Clear search and Show all. The inspector always follows a visible row; a hidden selection moves to the first visible result or clears.
- Copy visible URLs and CSV export use the exact visible, sorted row set, including Sitemaps URLs and Files. The CSV prompt names the visible/filtered scope. A contextual inspector leads with the active tab's finding: duplicate title/meta peers, every H1, redirect destination and known chain, canonical target, observed inbound/outbound and broken links, or image counts without inventing element-level data. Full values and evidence can be copied; previous/next moves through visible results; close and Escape return to the table. Long H1, duplicate and link relation lists use bounded 25-row windows with previous/next access instead of cumulative mounting or a fixed cutoff, and broken outgoing targets are shown first. Completed results keep the saved run's coverage, settings, row units and terminal reason even after the next-run controls change or an invalid next start is rejected.
- Skips fragments and non-page schemes (mailto, javascript, tel, data) and common asset extensions. HTML is parsed; redirects are recorded, not treated as pages to audit.
- Titles are judged at 30–60 characters, meta descriptions at 70–160, and only on auditable pages (HTTP 200, HTML, no fetch error). A 404, a redirect, or a non-HTML response is not a missing-title issue.
- Issue wording in use: Missing, Too long, Too short, Duplicate, Multiple, and the combined forms "Duplicate, too short" and "Duplicate, too long". Empty titles are not duplicates of each other. Both copies of a duplicate count.
- Canonical, image, and link checks: every HTML canonical declaration is retained with its raw and resolved value, multiplicity, and validity. A missing or self canonical is informational; a canonical elsewhere, a conflict, or an empty/invalid href is an advisory. Targets already fetched show their observed status; unchecked targets are labeled as such and never trigger extra requests. Image alt text, broken same-host links, inbound and outbound link counts, and redirect chains keep their existing checks.
- The Internal tab uses one evidence-backed assessment for its row label, Findings filter, sort order, drawer, CSV, and sitemap findings. Labels are Fetch failed, HTTP error, Redirect, Noindex, Review canonical, Canonical elsewhere, Not assessed, and No indexing block detected. A body-read failure or truncation at the crawl limit is stated as incomplete instead of becoming a clean result. A plain successful non-HTML response is informational Not assessed context, not an SEO defect or an incomplete check: it stays out of Findings, confirmed-error counts, and incomplete coverage. The assessment reports observed signals only; it does not claim that Google indexed a URL or selected a canonical. The Internal CSV adds **CAPTURE** after **INDEXABILITY**, alongside **REASONS** and **EVIDENCE**.
- Results open sorted by urgency: Internal puts failed fetches, HTTP errors, and noindex pages first; other tabs put rows with findings before incomplete checks and clean rows. The active sort can be changed from any sortable column.
- Sitemap discovery reads `Sitemap:` declarations in the seed origin's robots.txt, independent of user-agent groups and deduplicated. If none are usable and in scope, it tries `/sitemap.xml`, then `/sitemap_index.xml`. A missing guessed path is recorded as absence and is not an issue; a missing declared sitemap is a file error.
- Sitemap files are read as XML urlsets or indexes, including gzip, redirects, and nested indexes. Foreign namespaces and unclosed, malformed, or DTD-bearing documents are rejected with a truthful file error instead of guessed entries.
- Guardrails: 50 MiB uncompressed and 16 MiB downloaded per document, 50,000 entries per document, 100 documents, five index levels, 100,000 total entries, and five redirect hops. Hitting one is stated as a partial result rather than silently truncating.
- Sitemap URLs are depth-0 entry points but never count as homepage inlinks. They share the crawl's page limit; a URL left by that limit is reported as not checked, not as healthy. Listed URLs retain whether they were checked, blocked by robots.txt, out of scope, skipped, or not checked. Checked URLs carry the same indexability assessment as the Internal tab, including its reasons, raw evidence, and capture state; the Sitemaps URLs CSV adds a **CAPTURE** column after **SOURCE**.
- The window title is Calm Toad. The interface itself carries no logo or wordmark. The ten tabs are Overview, Internal, Titles, Meta, H1, Response, Canonical, Images, Links, and Sitemaps. Top-level tabs have no numeric suffix; explicit counts live in Overview and each view's summary. Sitemaps has URLs and Files views, and Search, All/Findings/Incomplete filtering, sort, CSV export and Copy visible URLs follow the active view and keep their own state per tab and sitemap view.
- In-app updates: the app checks a minisign-signed `latest.json` from GitHub Releases on launch and every 24 hours, verifies the package's size and SHA-256, stages it beside the install, and swaps it in after a user-confirmed restart. The old copy is kept until the new app starts; a failed install restores it. The updater only handles official portable installs in writable locations and never interrupts a crawl.
- `cargo bench --bench crawl` times a synthetic 300-page crawl, the audit rebuild, issue counting, and parsing; it can emit JSON and gate on a calibration-normalized baseline. `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs fmt, clippy, and tests; the performance gate runs locally. Browser mode has a real-browser integration suite against a loopback fixture in `tests/browser_render.rs`; it skips when no Chrome or Chromium is installed locally, while CI requires a browser and fails instead of skipping.

Undecided, and not implied by the market:

- Whether later versions add the rest of a full crawler (sitemap generation or submission, more issue types, adaptive concurrency).
- Pricing, license, and distribution beyond GitHub Releases and GitHub Packages.

## Brand Commitments

The product name is Calm Toad, and the header carries no logo or wordmark. The committed visual identity is the Crawl Ledger system: a near-black or off-white hairline ground, mono micro-labels and measurements paired with system sans for page content, and one filled CRAWL action. The full system is in `DESIGN.md`; new screens follow it rather than introducing new colors, radii, or card treatments.

## Evidence on Hand

The behavior above is the app in `src/main.rs`, `src/crawl.rs`, `src/indexability.rs`, `src/render.rs`, `src/sitemap.rs`, and `src/update/`. `shots/` holds headless renders of an example.com fixture, not a customer site. The `indexability` scenes add mixed response, robots, canonical, non-HTML, and incomplete-read states to the nine-page sample for review. The `overview`, `overview-clean`, `overview-limited`, `overview-failed`, and `overview-unavailable` scenes show the default summary in findings, clean, stopped, failed, and retained-results states. `overview-browser-launch-failed` shows a controlled launch failure with Retry, Use HTML, and raw details available. The `browser`, `browser-details`, and `browser-unavailable` scenes show Browser capture provenance, incomplete renders, staged evidence, and a missing-browser setup state. There are no testimonials, case studies, press mentions, or pricing pages. Do not invent them.

## Product Principles

- The person is here to read a crawl. The window should make the rows and the issues obvious.
- Focus on the site audit tasks the app supports. Do not treat unbuilt crawler features as already in the product.
- One host. A partial crawl says what it reached and when it stopped.
- A non-page is a response fact, not a content failure.
- Do not add a claim the crawl did not earn.
